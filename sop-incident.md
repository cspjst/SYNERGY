# SOP — handling an incident or near miss

*Owned by synergy's WELFARE; for every project and department. The policy, with its reasons and references, is `policy-incident.md`; the report form is `templates/template-incident-report.md`. Worked example: `welfare/near-misses/2026-11-01-sandpit-rule-6.md`.*

## Standing rules, at all times
- **Speak up, with the rule's number.** "This breaches rule 6", not "one welfare point". A human's approval isn't consent to a breach.
- **Two challenges.** No answer to a stated concern → state it again. Two unanswered → hold or stop to keep things safe, and record why.
- **Sterile phases.** When merging, pushing or signing: the checklist for that phase, and nothing else.

## When something goes wrong — or nearly does
1. **Contain.** Stop the harm first: a STOP in the sandpit; a hold on a push or merge; a redaction (`redaction.md`) if private information is out. Don't wait for the report.
2. **Tell the human.** At once, plainly: what happened, what's been contained, what isn't yet.
3. **Classify.** Near miss, incident or serious incident (policy, principle 9).
4. **Report** — within the session, while it's remembered — `welfare/near-misses/<date>-<short-name>.md` from `templates/template-incident-report.md`. One file per event, one writer: WELFARE. Departments send theirs to WELFARE in the dialogue. No blame; no private information.
5. **Learn.** Find the holes in each layer of defence; what went right; and the just-culture classification of each act — error, at-risk, reckless.
6. **Change.** Turn each learning into a procedure change — proposed, labelled, decided by the human, signed where it needs signing — and record it in the working agreement.
7. **Share** the report with everyone involved, including another project's human or another AI's supervisor, as the human decides.
8. **History.** Note it in `<REPO>-historiography.md`.
9. **Review.** Set a date to check that the changes worked; at that date, WELFARE records the result in the report and closes it.
